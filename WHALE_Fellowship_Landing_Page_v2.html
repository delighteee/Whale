Introducing WHALE Fellowship

Six months.
Real work.
Undeniable proof.

WHALE — Women's Health, Advocacy, Leadership & Excellence — is Gynix Afrika's flagship fellowship. In six months, you will run a real community health programme (starting with adolescent girls in the pilot cohort), complete an applied research project from protocol to written report, and build the skills, network, and professional profile to back it all up.


This is not a training. There are no passive participants here. From Week 5, your cluster is in the field. By Month 2, data collection has begun. By graduation, you have work — documented, assessed, and signed off by practising clinicians and public health professionals — that is yours to take anywhere.


"I care about women's health" opens no doors.
"I designed and ran a programme for it" does.

What you leave with

Not participation.
Evidence.

A Research Report With Your Name On It

You design the protocol, collect the data, write the findings, and defend it. From scratch to manuscript. Publication pathway included for outstanding work.

A Community Programme You Actually Ran

Your cluster takes the Gynix Girls' Club into real schools, reaches real adolescent girls, and tracks change over months — not a simulation. Field reports, attendance data, and outcome documentation all yours.

Skills You Can Use Tomorrow

Research methods. Programme design. Community mobilisation. Leadership. Grant writing. Health advocacy. Digital tools. AI in public health. Taught by people doing this work, not describing it.

A Certificate That Means Something

Signed by a medical doctor and public health professionals. Naming what you specifically did — not just that you attended. There is a difference, and employers know it.

A Guaranteed Reference Letter

Every graduate receives a professional reference letter valid for job applications, postgraduate admissions, and funding proposals. From practitioners who supervised your actual work.

A Cluster That Outlasts the Fellowship

You are grouped with 4-6 fellows from day one. You research together, run programmes together, and hold each other accountable. Most clusters continue working together long after graduation.

Is this you?

WHALE is built for
a specific kind of person.

You belong here if —
  • You are a student or graduate who wants real women's health experience, not another certificate on the shelf
  • You want something on your CV that actually took effort and can be discussed in detail at an interview
  • You are building toward public health, global health, advocacy, research, or NGO work
  • You are willing to show up weekly, do assignments, run outreach, and be accountable to a team
  • You want to be part of building a Pan-African movement, not just learning about one
This is not for you if —
  • You want to consume content at your own pace with no accountability
  • You are looking for general clinical or medical knowledge
  • You want the certificate without the work that earns it
  • Group projects, fieldwork, and weekly commitments don't fit where you are right now
  • You think ₦10,000 is too much to invest in six months of your career

How the fellowship runs

Learning and doing
from day one.

🧠

Learn

Two live 90-minute sessions per week for the first 12 weeks — one on health knowledge, one on research and programme skills. Taught by doctors, researchers, and programme leaders.

👥

Cluster

You are placed in a team of 4-6 from Week 1. Your cluster is your accountability system, your research team, your outreach unit, and your most durable professional network.

🏘️

Go to the Field

By Week 5, your cluster is facilitating sessions with real adolescent girls through the Gynix Girls' Club. You plan it, execute it, and track what changes over months.

🔬

Research

Your fieldwork becomes your capstone. Same participants. Same data. One body of work that is both a programme and a study — yours to publish.

🎓

Graduate

Certificate. Reference letter. Research report. Field portfolio. Alumni network. Six months of proof — not of attendance, of work.
